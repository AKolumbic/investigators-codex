PROLOGUE — INTO ARKHAM


The year is 1930.

Several months ago, the stock market fell. Not the way a leaf falls — the way a man does when he steps from a ledge after witnessing the shattering of his dreams. It happened in October, and the falling has not stopped. Banks have bolted their doors. Factories stand dark as mausoleums. Men who built their lives over thirty years have watched them come apart in thirty days. There are lines outside soup kitchens now — long lines, quiet lines, full of people who never imagined they would be standing in one. Overnight, the American dream has become the American nightmare.

It is May, and it is raining — the kind of rain that does not fall so much as settle in, like something that followed you home and will not leave. It has been raining for what feels like a very long time. Twenty-three miles northeast of Boston, past the last places anyone would think to look for anything, the land turns strange. The hills fold in on themselves. The trees grow close and old and keep their own counsel. The Miskatonic River cuts through all of it — running high and dark, gorged on weeks of runoff, its surface the color of coffee gone cold. It moves slowly, deliberately, the way something moves when it knows exactly where it is going and is in no hurry to get there.

Follow that river long enough and it will bring you to Arkham, Massachusetts — a town that sits on its banks the way a headstone sits on a grave, marking something buried underneath. The university still stands, though its coffers are thinner. The mills still run, though half their windows are dark. The rain washes down through the streets and into the Miskatonic, and whatever the town does not want to look at, the river takes away.

Into this place — this tired, wet, frightened place — four strangers are arriving. They do not know each other. Not yet. Each of them has received a letter from the same man.

His name is Victor Valentine. A private investigator out of Boston. He came to Arkham asking questions — the kind of questions that Arkham does not like.
